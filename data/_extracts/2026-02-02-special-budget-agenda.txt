Draft 10 Year Capital Forecast [2026 – 2035] and Reserve Continuity Analysis  
 
The Chief Administrative Officer and Treasurer/Director of Corporate Services 
recommend: 
 
1) THAT FIN Report No. 03-2026 be received;  
 
2) AND THAT Council adopt in-principle the Ten Year Capital Forecast [2026 – 
2035] which is be utilized to inform the Township of North Dumfries’ future 
financial Capital Budgets and ongoing planning / financial analysis 
 
1. Purpose 
 
The purpose of this Report is to provide Council with background information on the 
draft 10 Year Capital Forecast [2026 – 2035] and the associated Reserve Continuity 
Analysis.  
 
The draft 2026 Budget document is posted on the Township’s website 
[www.northdumfries.ca] for review / downloading – follow the link to the 2026 Budget.  
 
The draft Ten Year Forecast (2026 – 2035) and the Reserve Continuity Analysis is 
included as Attachment No. 1 to this Report.  
 
2. Background 
 
As part of the annual Budget cycle, Council reviews the Ten-year Capital Forecast.   
The last review occurred on August 13th, 2025 in a Special Council Meeting.    
 
The results of the review and direction from the August 2025 meeting regarding the 
2026 - 2035 projects were updated and have been brought forward by Staff into the 
draft 2026 Budget.   In addition, where appropriate, adjustments have been made to 
some projected expenditures to align with new cost information and/or changes to 
service standards.   
 
It is important to note that the 10 Year Capital Forecast is a living document and is 
subject to change based on new and improved information being received by Staff.

The 10 Year Forecast is meant to reflect the direction of the Township’s needs in terms 
of the delivery of the long term Capital projects to be undertaken during the identified 
time period [2026 – 2035].  The listing of Capital Projects is based upon key aspects of 
the Township’s Asset Management Plan for the renewal of major assets (i.e.:  rolling 
stock, buildings, parks); the rehabilitation / renewal of the linear infrastructure network 
(i.e.:  roads, bridges, sidewalks, etc.); implications arising from residential and non-
residential growth; community expectations; and, the evolving circumstances and 
structural changes occurring in the local and the global economy.   
 
The review session of the Long-Term Capital Financial Plans at the February 2, 2026 
Special Council (Budget) Meeting has been designed to align with the following goals: 
 
1. Provide an updated outline of the 10-year forecast to assist Staff in the 
development of the 2026 – 2035 projects listing and the alignment of the capital 
delivery program with Council expectations;  
 
2. To facilitate a roundtable discussion amongst Council on the long-term capital 
projects forecast for 2026 and beyond as several major items will affect the 2027 
Budget and future Staff workplans.  It is important for the Township to prepare for 
those items prior to preparing for the 2027 Budget and to consider the 
implications during the 2026 work term; and,  
 
3. Inform the need to allocate Reserve Transfers during the annual Budget review 
process to ensure that funding is available during the 10 year Capital forecast 
period to deliver the range of projects identified.  
 
The longer-term objectives behind the preparation of a Ten-Year Capital Forecast (2026 
– 2035) have a multitude of reasons, namely:   
 
• Identifying and prioritizing projects;   
 
• Assists in setting future annual Budgets. This will facilitate pre-planning of 
financing that is required to fund the Capital Forecast by either setting aside 
Reserves, planning for Debt financing, and/or shifting or deferring projects to 
avoid rate spikes or funding shortfall;  
 
• If necessary, sequencing integrated projects to ensure efficiencies;  
 
• Establishing an understanding of cost exposures and the need / basis to fund 
Reserves and to set alternate funding sources such as fees & charges to prepare 
for the future expenditure;

10 Year Capital Forecast -  Aggressive Program and Significant Fiscal Implications 
 
The current draft 10 Year Capital Forecast (2026 to 2035) has established an 
aggressive campaign intermixing a blend of new assets in addition to on-going repair & 
replacement of existing infrastructure, rolling stock and buildings.    
 
The on-going commitments by Council to grow Reserve Transfers to fund the 10 Year 
Capital Forecast for the 2026 – 2035 time period has established a program that will 
require further borrowing either internally or issuance of new debt in order to achieve 
the program.   
 
The draft 10 Year Forecast leaves very little uncommitted funds to complete new and 
yet undefined projects without Council either further increasing fund transfers to 
Reserves above the model presently developed; altering the scope of the project 
deliverables; dropping projects or pushing projects outside of the 10 year horizon; 
increasing commitments to Debt financing; and/or being successful in securing new 
Grant opportunities from Senior Levels of Government. 
 
In addition, in the later years of the draft 10 Year Forecast, the majority of the funds 
raised to be transferred to Reserves for future projects are actually being allocated to 
project in the same year that the funds are raised or in the next fiscal year. In the mid-
part of the forecast, some reserves are almost completely drawn down to zero.  This 
results in very little growth to many of the Township’s Capital Reserves, and by 
extension, this translates into very little flexibility to accommodate capital cost overruns 
(estimates vs actual bid price) and/or adding new capital projects.  
 
Council also needs to assess that the Development Charges component of various 
Public Works projects, including road reconstruction projects assigned to accommodate 
growth.   The present delivery timeline for road related projects and the projected off-
setting annual Development Charges revenue will establish a shortfall in the pooled 
reserves attributed to Development Charges within 5 years and continues for the 
balance of the 10 Year Forecast .  As illustrated in Schedule F Reserve Continuity, the 
Development Charges pooled account is in a negative cash flow position for the 
majority of the time period extending from 2030 to 2035.    
 
The Township is scheduled to update the Development Charges Background Report 
and implementing Bylaw in 2026.   This updated Study will account for Development 
Charges and eligible recoveries of capital costs attributed to growth and it may resolve 
the cash flow predicament as highlighted in Schedule F.   Alternatively, the updated

C.    Next Steps – Budget 2026 
 
As part of the 2026 Budget exercise, the Ten-Year Capital Forecast (2026 to 2035) is 
not proposed to be approved and implemented.   Staff will be seeking the adoption of 
the framework of the Ten-Year Forecast for the purposes of continued study, analysis 
and guiding principles, for both the expenditures and revenues.  As such, Council is 
adopting in principle the Capital Forecast  
 
The Ten-Year Capital Forecast will be reviewed again in August 2026, in preparation of 
the 2027 Budget and to ensure that representative projects have been properly 
captured and that the projects are prioritized in a fashion consistent with expectations.   
This will form part of the annual update and review.    
 
D.  February 2, 2026 Council Review of Long Term Capital Plan –  Tasks 
 
Staff would request that Council review the draft Ten-Year Capital Forecast and be 
prepared as part of the February 2, 2026 Special Council (Budget) Meeting to identify / 
confirm projects for inclusion in the 2026-2035 Capital Forecast going forward.  
 
The draft Ten-Year Capital Forecast with the estimated $150,479,522 in aggregate 
expenditures is not fully funded.  It is important for Council to identify the key projects, 
principally focused within Public Works Division (Linear Infrastructure) and Leisure & 
Community Services Department (Facilities and Parks/Trails) that represent the 
priorities of Council.    
 
Arising from this review and discussion during Budget deliberations, Council may: 
 
o drop projects; and/or,  
 
o modify or scope projects; and/or,  
 
o delay or extend the timeline for the completion of projects; and/or  
 
o add new projects; and/or 
 
o identify projects that would only be completed if funding partnerships could 
be attained with Senior Levels of Government and/or other parties.

Rate increase.

 Part IV Heritage Designations – Ontario Heritage Act  
 
In accordance with Provincial legislation Municipalities are to address properties listed 
in the equivalent of the Township’s Built Heritage Inventory that are deemed suitable for 
consideration for a Part IV designation under the Ontario Heritage Act by the end of 
December 31st, 2026.    Any properties that are not designated under Part IV of the Act 
on January 1st, 2027 are to be removed from the Built Heritage Inventory list. 
 
The Heritage Advisory Committee and Staff have presented update Reports to Council 
on the status of work related to the pursuit of Part IV Heritage Designations for various 
properties across North Dumfries through the Summer and Fall of 2025.  
 
In November 2025 the Advisory Committee hosted a public forum with invitations sent 
to approximately 95 property owners with approximately 45 persons in attendance.    
Arising from the forum, there are approximately 12 to 16 property owners that have 
identified the potential interest in pursuing a Part IV designation.     
 
The intent of the Committee in the Winter of 2026 is to finalize a listing of potential 
properties for a Part IV consideration and, to then retain a Consultant to pursue the 
detailed analysis of the potential heritage resources and to “score” the resource against 
the criteria of Ontario Regulation 09/96.     The Consultant, on properties that achieve a 
minimum of two (2) of the criteria would then undertake the preparation of a summary of 
the heritage assets / elements to be protected that would in time be included as a 
Schedule to the implementing Bylaw to designate a property.

properties may be advanced for a Part IV consideration.    The cost to retain the

per property.   At a mid-point cost of $5,250 (plus HST) the projected cost to undertaken 
the analysis would be $74,795

and the purchase of plaques that would be affixed to the designated building.    The

By-law Number No. 3618-26 
 
Being a By-law to Confirm the Proceedings of Council of the Corporation of the 
Township of North Dumfries at this Meeting held on February 2, 2026. 
 
 
Whereas Section 5 (3) of the Municipal Act, 2001 S.O. 2001, c.25, as amended 
provides that a municipal power, including a municipality’s capacity, rights, powers 
and privileges under section 9, shall be exercised by by-law unless the municipality is 
specifically authorized to do otherwise; 
 
And Whereas Section 9 of the said Municipal Act, 2001 provides that a municipality 
has the capacity, rights, powers and privileges of a natural person for the purpose of 
exercising its authority under this or any other Act; 
 
And Whereas Section 11 (1) of the said Municipal Act provides that A lower-tier 
municipality and an upper-tier municipality may provide any service or thing that the 
municipality considers necessary or desirable for the public, subject to the rules set 
out in subsection (4) ; 
 
And Whereas in many cases action which is taken or authorized to be taken by 
Council does not lend itself to the passage of an individual by-law; 
 
And Whereas it is deemed desirable and expedient that the actions of the Council 
as herein set forth be adopted, ratified and confirmed by by-law; 
 
Now Therefore the Council of the Corporation of the Township of North 
Dumfries Enacts as Follows: 
 
1. THAT the action of the Council at its  Council Meeting held on February 2, 
2026 in respect to each report, motion, resolution or other action passed and 
taken by the Council at its meeting, is hereby adopted, ratified , and 
confirmed, as if each resolution or other action was adopted, ratified , and 
confirmed by separate by-law. 
 
2. THAT the Mayor and the proper officers of the Township are hereby 
authorized and directed to do all things necessary to give effect to the said 
action, or to obtain approvals where required, and, except where otherwise 
provided, the Mayor and the Clerk are her eby directed to execute all 
documents necessary in that behalf and to affix the corporate seal of the 
Township to all such documents. 
 
 
Read a First, Second and Third Time and Finally Passed this 2nd day of 
February, 2026.
