8.1.1 Report No.01-2026: Proposed Zoning By-law Amendment for 92 
Northumberland Street (File No. ZC-08/25).  
 
Micheal Campos, Manager of Planning provides Council with an overview of the 
application. The purpose of the application is to establish site-specific zoning for 
the property to facilitate the development of an underutilized portion of the 
property for a four-storey, 33-unit residential apartment building intended to be 
offered as rental units, while retaining the existing church on the property.

The subject property is municipally addressed 92 Northumberland Street in the 
Township of North Dumfries. The property fronts onto Northumberland Street, 
opposite Hall Street, and abuts the Nith River system to the west. The subject 
property is approximately 0.88 hectares in area and has a corresponding frontage 
of approximately 45.8 metres onto Northumberland Street. 
 
Questions from the Public: 
 
Glen Wellings, agent has registered to attend the meeting. Mr. Wellings provides 
and overview of the application, notes that there is still work to be done and 
indicates that they are looking to resolve concerns and issues related to this 
application. It is further noted that the application is consistent with Provincial 
Planning Statement and is available to answer any questions.  
Michael Stumpf has registered to attend the meeting. Mr. Stumpf notes his 
opposition to the application and indicates the following concerns: construction will 
cause noise and dust that will impact his day-to-day life, increased volume in 
traffic, impacts to his ability to enter and exit their driveway, parking demand, 
height and scale of building, parking lot light pollution, impact on his daily use of 
this property.  
 
Julia Bernier has registered to attend the meeting. Ms. Bernier notes her 
opposition to the application and indicates the following concerns: how the 
application aligns with the PPE, public safety, health concerns, increased traffic, 
site access, waste management and recycling, community safety and wellbeing 
concerns. 
 
Glenyce Maus has registered to attend the meeting. The following concerns are 
noted: volume of traffic, drawings do not include a sidewalk, signage, lack of 
parking, snow removal concerns and limited access point.  
 
Bonnie Brown has registered to attend the meeting. It is noted that she agrees the 
Township needs affordable housing, she is opposed to this project. It is further 
noted that she has parking concerns. as most people own their own vehicle due to 
lack of public transportation in the Township. 
 
Peggy Unger has registered to attend the meeting. The following concerns are 
noted: four story building does not fit into the community, proposed building will 
block the view of the Nith River, proposed building may cause shadowing, lack of 
parking in the downtown area, lack of infrastructure and safety concerns. 
 
Questions from Council:   
 
Councillor Wilms inquiries what the shared parking model looks like. Staff note 
that those details have not been explored as of yet.

8.1.2 Report No. PD-05-2026: Proposed Zoning By-law Amendment for 3027 
Cedar Creek Road (File No. ZC-07/25).  
Micheal Campos, Manager of Planning provides Council with an overview of the 
application. The purpose of the application is to rezone the lands in order to 
facilitate the development of the lands for a warehouse and truck terminal 
(transport and logistics facility) on the eastern portion of the property, while 
conserving the adjacent environmental features along the western half of the 
property. 
 
The subject property is municipally addressed as 3027 Cedar Creek Road. The 
property is situated on the south side of Cedar Creek Road, between 
Northumberland Street and the Cedar Creek Road/Highway 401 interchange, with 
Highway 401 forming the southern boundary of the site.

development, supporting studies that are supportive of the proposal and land use 
framework.

comments.

Corporation with a new Sponsors Council that would lose its corporate status 
and independent resources; and,

municipal employers and employees to be made without meaningful 
municipal oversight, increasing financial risk for municipalities and local 
taxpayers; and,

taxes or local services;

Firearm Discharge By-law Update  
        
Recommendation 
 
The Senior Municipal Law Enforcement Officer recommends: 
 
1. THAT BLE Report No. 01-2025 be received; 
 
2. AND THAT Staff be authorized to initiate a public consultation and 
engagement process to solicit input from interested members of the 
community; 
 
3. AND THAT Staff return to the April 27, 2026, meeting with a follow up Report 
to Council. 
 
1. Purpose 
 
The purpose of this Report is to present to Council for consideration an update to Firearms 
Discharge By-Law No. 700-83. 
 
2. Background  
 
It is appropriate to review current Bylaws to: 
 
i) assess against current legislation; 
ii)  ensure that the Bylaw continues to address their intended purpose; and,  
ii) evaluate against the community’s needs and expectations.  
 
The Township’s current Firearms Discharge By-law, Bylaw 700-83, has been in effect since 
1983.   The current Firearms Discharge By-law was passed by Council on October 17, 1983.  
Short Form Wording and Set Fines were never approved. 
 
3. Options and Analysis  
 
After reviewing the current Firearms Discharge By-law, staff are recommending changes to the 
By-law that would prohibit all firearms and bows within the various settlement areas across 
North Dumfries.   It would also keep the current prohibited area around the  rare Charitable 
Research Reserve.

The following changes are being recommended by Staff in the rewrite of the current Firearms 
Discharge By-law:  
 
1. Adding updated definitions to Section 2 of the By-law;  
 
2. Bringing the By-law up to date with current legislation as the legislation in the current 
By-law is outdated; 
 
3. Removing the ambiguous “Schedule A”  within the existing Bylaw  that is written in 
metes and bounds and replacing it with mapping  showing the rare Charitable 
Research Reserve land; 
 
4. Prohibiting the discharge of bows and firearms within all settlement areas of North 
Dumfries; 
 
5. Adding provisions around schools, places of worship, day nurserie s, recreation 
centres and parks to establish a minimum separation distance; 
 
6. Providing exemptions for blank ammunition, future businesses such as gun clubs 
and paintball arenas and allowing landowners to protect their land from threatening 
wildlife.

with the community to solicit comments and input.

3. INTERPRETATION 
3.1 The regulations established in this By-law apply to the discharge of Bows and Firearms 
on all lands and waters within the boundaries of the Township. 
3.2 The attached Schedules 1 to 4 form part of this By-law. 
3.3 Nothing in this By-law shall be interpreted as reducing or eliminating the need for full 
compliance with the provisions of all Federal and Provincial statutes or regulations relating 
to Bows and Firearms. 
4. REGULATIONS 
 Settlement Areas 
4.1 No person shall discharge, cause to be discharged, or allow to be discharged a Bow or 
Firearm in the Settlement Areas of the Township. 
Regulated Area 1 
4.2 No person shall discharge, cause to be discharged, or allow to be discharged a Bow or 
Firearm in Regulated Area 1 of the Township. 
Compliance 
4.3 No person shall discharge, cause to be discharged, or allow to be discharged a Bow or 
Firearm on any private property on which the discharge of Bows and Firearms are permitted 
unless he or she; 
 a) is the owner or occupier of the property, or 
 b) has the express written permission of the owner or occupier of the property. 
5. GENERAL 
Although the provisions of section 4.3 allow for the discharge of firearms in certain specified 
circumstances, no person shall discharge, cause to be discharged or allow to be discharged 
a firearm in the Township; 
a) on any property owned or occupied by the Township, without express written 
permission by the Township;  
b) on any property owned by the Region of Waterloo or the Grand River 
Conservation Authority without express written permission to do so; 
b) on or within 150 metres, or at any target within 150 metres, of any property used 
for the purposes of a school, place of worship, day nursery, community recreation 
centre or park;  
c) on, across or within 30 metres of any highway, private road or recreational trail; 
d) for the purpose of detonating “Tannerite” (also known as “Shockwave”) or any 
other binary explosive that requires a shot by a firearm to initiate a detonation.

6. EXEMPTIONS 
Despite the provisions of sections 4 & 5 of this by-law, nothing in the by-law shall serve to 
prohibit the discharge of a Firearm by:  
a) A Law Enforcement Officer in the lawful execution of their duties.  
b) Any person, while lawfully discharging a firearm at a rod and gun club, gun club, 
skeet club or a shooting range which is operating in compliance with all federal and 
provincial legislation and all municipal by-laws and wherein all necessary and 
property safety precautions have been implemented to the satisfaction of the 
Waterloo Regional Police and full approval has been obtained by the Chief Firearms 
Officer of Ontario.  
c) A person firing blank ammunition which does not discharge a projectile, for or in 
connection with its lawful use, in any of the following activities; a motion picture, 
television or stage production, historical re-enactment or display, starting or 
controlling a sporting event or in connection with special honour guard tribute by 
the Royal Canadian Legion or similar community based event, provided that the 
person has obtained Township approval and any appropriate permits.  
d) An establishment or business providing “paint ball gaming” , at a location zoned 
for this purpose and duly authorized pursuant to the Township’s Zoning By-law, as 
amended. 
e) A person who is an owner or occupier of lands who believes, on reasonable 
grounds, that Wildlife is threatening to injure that persons property, including, but 
not limited to crops, livestock, pets or persons on that property.

By-law Enforcement Dispatch Agreement with the Waterloo Regional Police Service  
 
Recommendation 
 
The Senior Municipal Law Enforcement Officer recommends: 
 
1. THAT BLE Report No. 02-2026 be received; 
 
2. AND THAT Council adopts the By-law Enforcement Dispatch Agreement with 
the Waterloo Regional Police Service (WRPS), the City of Kitchener, the City 
of Waterloo and the City of Cambridge, included as Attachment No. 1 to this 
Report, subject to any minor revisions as required by Township Legal 
Counsel and/or the Chief Administrative Officer;  
 
3. AND THAT the Mayor and Clerk be authorized to execute the By-law 
Enforcement Dispatch Agreement with the Waterloo Regional Police Service, 
subject to the  provision that the Cities of Kitchener, Waterloo and 
Cambridge also participate in the program. 
1.  Purpose 
 
The purpose of this Report is to present for the consideration of Council the framework of an 
Agreement for the provision of dispatch services with the Waterloo Regional Police Service in 
support of By-law Enforcement Staff and its operation and, to seek the approval of Council to 
execute the Agreement. 
2.  Background 
 
The Waterloo Regional Police Service (WRPS) has been dispatching By -law Enforcement 
Officers within the three (3) Cities for close to 30 years.  Until now, there has been no cost 
associated with the provision of the dispatch services. 
 
Recent amendments to the Community Safety and Policing Act permit Police Service Boards 
to recover costs for non -core services.   The provision of dispatch services in support of the 
Area Municipalities and specifically By -law Enforcement personnel  would be included within 
the realm of non-core services. 
 
In the summer of 2024 WRPS initiated introductory discussions concerning the provision of 
Dispatch Services in support of the Area Municipalities.   In the Fall of 2025, a panel was

this Report.  
3.  Options and Analysis  
 
Staff have been working with the WRPS and our municipal counterparts with the Cities of 
Cambridge, Kitchener and Waterloo to bring forward an Agreement for the consideration of 
the respective Councils and the Police Services Board.   The draft Agreement is included as 
Attachment No. 1 to this Report. 
 
Some of the key highlights as set out in the Agreement are as follows: 
 
i) The Agreement will become effective July 1st, 2026; 
 
ii) WRPS will be responsible for hiring and training of the staff dedicated to By-law 
Enforcement dispatch, providing the dedicated radio channel, answering and 
dispatching calls for service, logging and tracking calls in CAD, generating 
occurrences numbers and monitoring Bylaw Enforcement Officers while on 
scene at calls for service; 
 
iii) WRPS will be responsible to maintain and operate the core infrastructure 
network in support of Bylaw Enforcement services;  
 
iv) If a Call for Service is received outside the hours during which the relevant 
Municipality has Municipal By-law Enforcement Officers on duty, the Dispatcher 
shall log an Occurrence that will not be dispatched until that Municipality has a 
Municipal By-law Enforcement Officer on duty and logged onto the Radio 
Channel; 
v) By-law Enforcement staff will be responsible for adhering to the radio protocols 
as set out by WRPS, logging on and off with police dispatch when on duty and 
providing a cell phone number for back up; 
 
vi) Dedicated Bylaw Enforcement Dispatchers will be available 24/7, 365 days a 
year; 
 
vii) Billing for service will be based on call volume with North Dumfries coming in at 
0.24% or approx. $2,700 for 2027. (estimated annual cost);

that monitors our personnel when on shift which provides a dimension of safety.   Having a 
direct link with police dispatch increases officer safety, provides guidance on properties that 
have a known risk or premise flags and assists in determining if By-law Enforcement Officers 
should be responding alone or with the back-up of the Police Service.

Fees for service will not start until October of 2026 and will be minimal for North Dumfries.

Budget. 
 
Based on 2025 call volumes, the fees for 2027 will be 0.24% of the overall annual cost of 
$1,132,700 or $2,718.28 applicable to the Township.   
 
Cost will increase slightly from year to year  based upon CPI or if there is a drastic increase 
in call volumes attributed to North Dumfries.

3.   Options and Analysis  
 
3.1 Overview & Context 
 
As noted in Section 270 (1) of the Municipal Act, municipalities are to establish a series of 
policies to assist with governance.    These policies are designed by their nature to ensure 
consistency in how the decision making process is undertaken and, to be accountable and 
transparent in terms of how information is published or made accessible.  
 
3.2 Accountability & Transparency Policy 
 
Included as Attachment No. 1 to this Report is a draft Accountability & Transparency Policy.   
This Policy is designed to align with the applicable provisions of Section 270 of the Municipal 
Act and, to ensure that internal provisions are established to ensure that the decision making 
process is accountable and transparent to the public.  
 
The Accountability & Transparency Policy outlines in Procedure 5 of the document the 
various measures to be deployed by the municipality to ensure accountability and 
transparency.    Matters identified include:  
 
 Financial 
 Internal Governance 
 Public Participation 
 Disclosure of Information  
 Procedural Bylaw 
 Open Meetings 
 Code of Conduct for Members of Council and Local Boards

3.3 Recruitment & Hiring Policy  
 
Included as Attachment No. 2 to this Report is a draft Recruitment & Hiring Policy.   This 
Policy is designed to align with the applicable provisions of Section 270 of the Municipal Act 
and, to is to establish a publicly articulated process for the recruitment and hiring of staff for 
either  vacant positions or the recruitment to hire for new positions are undertaken in a fair 
and consistent manner.  
 
It is an overarching objective that hiring is undertaken based on merit and ability to perform 
the identified tasks associated with the posted position. 
 
The Policy, as drafted, establishes a program that outlines the applicable procedures of how 
municipal positions are to be advertised, recruited, assessed and on-boarded.

Recruitment & Hiring Policy.

on-boarding of new Staff as well as refresher training for existing personnel.  
 
Staff continue to work on three additional Policies at this time which will be included in a 
future Report to Council.   The three Policies include:  
 
 Delegation of powers and duties 
 How the municipality will protect and enhance tree canopy and natural vegetation 
 Communication and how to provide notice to the public
